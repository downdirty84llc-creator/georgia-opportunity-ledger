## 1. Why this campaign and not another

Three facts from the register decide this:

- **Proven revenue is invoiced service work at $350.79 average**, not self-serve
  digital products. Ten invoices, three customers, $3,507.86 — and $0 from the
  storefront across 574 sessions.
- **The audience does not know DD84 exists.** Seven search visits in 180 days,
  84% of traffic direct. This is not a persuasion problem, it is a reach problem.
- **A perfect entry product already exists and is badly placed:** _Remote Tune
  Readiness Review_, $49 basic / $75 with datalog check. It is on the home-page
  collection — but **eighth of ten**, behind PCM removal labour, a Pops & Bangs
  add-on and three deposits. The cheapest, easiest first purchase in the
  catalogue is sitting below four things that only make sense to someone who has
  already decided to buy.

So the campaign does not sell tuning. **It sells the question people already ask
themselves before they spend $500** — *is my build actually ready?* — and answers
it for $49.

## 3. Creative — angle A: "The datalog tells you. Most people can't read it."

Leads with competence, not discount. For technical forums and long-form posts.

**Headline options**

- Your cam swap didn't ruin the idle. The VE table did.
- Before you pay for a tune, find out if the car is ready for one.
- Most "bad tunes" are a wiring fault nobody checked.

**Long post / video description**

> Half the "my tune is bad" posts I read are not tuning problems.
>
> They're a lean cylinder from a vacuum leak, a MAF that's reading fine at idle
> and lying at 4,000 RPM, a ground that drops half a volt under load, or a
> cam card that never made it into the VE table.
>
> A tuner who takes your money without checking that first is going to hand the
> car back running exactly the same.
>
> Send me the datalog and the build sheet. I'll tell you what the data actually
> shows, what's mechanical versus calibration, and whether the car is ready to
> tune — before anyone touches a table. $49.
>
> If it turns out the car is fine and just needs calibration, I'll say that too.

**Short ad copy (under 125 characters)**

- Send the datalog. Find out what's actually wrong before you pay for a tune. $49.
- Cam swap idle bad? It might not be the tune. $49 readiness review.

**Call to action:** _Send your datalog — $49 readiness review_

**Image brief:** A real datalog screenshot on a laptop in a garage, one channel
circled by hand. No stock photos of shiny engine bays. The credibility is in
looking like actual work.

**The real cause was not the order, it was the sort.** The Home page collection
was set to `BEST_SELLING`, and the store has **never had an order**. With no

## 6. Lead data model

Every buyer and every enquiry becomes a record with these fields. Shopify
customer tags carry it until there is a real CRM.

| Group         | Fields                                                                           |
| ------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Identity      | Name, email, preferred contact                                                   |
| Source        | `campaign`, `angle`, `channel`, and the post or thread it came from              |
| Vehicle       | Year/make/model, engine, ECU/PCM, transmission, power adder                      |
| Need          | Symptom in their words, what they've already tried                               |
| Qualification | Budget signal, urgency, whether the car currently runs, whether they can datalog |
| Value         | Entry purchase, and the realistic core-service value if it converts              |
| Status        | new → review delivered → quoted → won / lost / nurture                           |
| Next action   | Specific action, owner, due date                                                 |

**Zero-lead-loss rule.** No qualified enquiry sits without a status, a value, an
owner, a next action and a date.

## 7. Follow-up — the part that turns $49 into $450

This is where the money is, and it is the step most people skip.

**On purchase (same day):** confirm, and ask for exactly what's needed — datalog,
build sheet, cam card, ECU/PCM type. Nothing else. Every extra question loses
people.

**On delivery of the review (the core moment):** deliver the finding in writing,
in plain language, split into three lists:

1. **Mechanical — fix before tuning.** Specific, with what to check.
2. **Calibration — this is what a tune actually fixes.**
3. **Fine as-is.** Say what's already right. It builds more trust than the
   problems do.

Then, and only then, one sentence: _"If you want me to do the calibration side,
here's what that looks like and what it costs."_ No pressure, no countdown.

**Day 3, if no reply:** one message. "Any questions on the findings?" Nothing else.

**Day 10:** if they said they'd fix mechanical issues first — "How did the
[specific item] turn out?" This is a real question about their car, not a
follow-up. It is also the highest-converting message in the sequence.

**Day 30:** move to long-term nurture. Stop unless they re-engage.

**Never:** guarantee a power figure, a result, or that a tune will fix a problem
that has not been diagnosed. §23.

## 9. Operations

- **Response time is the offer.** A readiness review that takes a week is worth
  nothing. Commit to a turnaround you can actually hold — and if you can't hold
  48 hours, say 5 days and beat it.
- **Publishing cadence:** answer real questions in real threads. Do not
  broadcast. One genuinely useful reply in an LS1Tech or HP Tuners thread beats
  ten promotional posts, and it is the only approach that survives forum rules.
- **Forum compliance:** most technical forums ban unmarked solicitation. Lead
  with the answer, disclose that you do this for a living, and link only when
  it's relevant. §23 — protect the brand from spammy marketing.
- **Escalation:** anything involving a customer's safety-critical fault, or a
  claim about emissions equipment, stops and gets reviewed before publishing.
