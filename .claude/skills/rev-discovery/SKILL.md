# Rev — Discovery Pass

Produces an **opportunity register**: verified facts, a scored queue, and
twelve-field approval briefs. It ends at §4 stage 4 — request approval. It
executes nothing.

## 1. Evidence first, ideas second

Do not brainstorm before you have looked. A register built on assumption is
worthless, and the most valuable findings in every pass so far came from reading
a live system, not from thinking harder.

Gather from whatever is connected:

- **Revenue** — Stripe charges: totals, customer concentration, average value,
  **and the date of the last payment.** A business that stopped earning is a
  completely different problem from one that never started.
- **Storefront** — Shopify orders, sessions, funnel steps, traffic sources over
  time. Look at the _trend_, not the total: a launch spike followed by silence
  tells you something a 12-month sum hides.
- **Catalogue** — products, prices, what is actually sellable, where the entry
  offer sits.
- **The application** — for the Ledger, whether it can take money at all.

Write down what you could **not** check, and why.

## 2. Find the governing constraint

**Before scoring anything, ask: what single thing is blocking revenue?**

Every pass so far has found one, and it was never what the brief assumed:

- The Ledger could not take payment at all — checkout returned 409 for every
  paid tier. Every marketing idea was worthless until that was fixed.
- DD84 Tuning had real revenue that _stopped eleven months ago_, then launched a
  storefront that has never sold anything. The constraint was not the store.

If the governing constraint is that the business cannot transact, or that nobody
can find it, **say so as the headline and shape the whole register around it.**
Do not bury it under a list of campaign ideas.

## 3. Score honestly (§9)

Ten factors, components always shown. Score what the evidence supports, not what
makes a favoured idea win. A low score on a good idea is information.

Apply the priority rule — urgent revenue recovery and approved work lead
regardless of score — and say when you have.

## 4. Write the register

To `docs/growth/YYYY-MM-DD-<venture>-register.md`:

1. **Verified facts table**, every row citing its source.
2. **The finding that governs everything else** — the constraint, stated plainly.
3. **Scored queue** with all components visible.
4. **Full twelve-field briefs** for the top three.
5. **Summaries** for the rest.
6. **Open questions you could not close**, and what would close them.

## 5. Rules that make the register trustworthy

- **Do not propose paid advertising into a funnel that cannot transact.** It
  converts at zero. Say why you are not proposing it.
- **Cheapest, fastest revenue first.** Reactivating a customer who already paid
  beats any acquisition campaign, and costs nothing.
- **No customer names in the file.** Customers are A, B, C.
- **Label every projection as a projection.** Where a base is too small to
  extrapolate from — three customers is too small — say that instead of
  producing a forecast.
- **Correct yourself in writing.** If this pass contradicts an earlier register,
  strike the old claim through, keep the reasoning that drove it, and explain
  what changed. Those corrections are the most useful part of the document.

## 6. Stop at the gate

End with what you are asking for: which briefs, in what order, and why. Then
stop. Do not execute, publish, spend or message anyone — even if the case seems
obvious. The gate is the product.
